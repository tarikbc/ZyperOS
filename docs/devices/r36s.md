# The R36S: a start

The R36S is a cheap vertical handheld with the GKD Pixel 2's chip, the
Rockchip RK3326. ZyperOS has a test card for it, which one tester has run
(below). It is not a release device yet: no release, ZyperOS Installer or
zyperos.com has it, and the test card is built from the source (below, "A
test card"). This page is for whoever picks it up: what is done, what is known
about the hardware and where each fact comes from, what is untested, and
what is left, in order.

I have no R36S myself. The R36S facts below come from the sources they name,
and from that first test.

## Tried on a real R36S

Sylver built the R36S branch and ran it on his unit, whose board has
"R36S-V12 (2023-08-18)" printed on it (2026-11-23). R36S-V12 is one of the
genuine boards' names in dArkOS's list (below, "Clones"). He ran two cards:
the first, then one with the first one's fixes and the sticks.

| Part | Result |
| --- | --- |
| The screen | black with the card's device tree; ZyperOS showed once he switched to the device tree for "panel 4". The second card had his overlay for it, `overlays/panel-4.dtbo`, picked with `zy_panel=4` |
| Brightness | every level works, the three boost steps over the top too, with the gamma table ZyperOS's R36S tree adds (ROCKNIX's tree gives the display none) |
| Screen Colors, Night Mode | work |
| Sound | works, the volume too |
| Buttons | work, L1, L2, R1 and R2 too |
| The analog sticks | work in PlayStation games (Diatom's R36S port reads them, and L3 and R3). In the launcher the left stick moves as the d-pad |
| The start's animation | not shown: neither ZyperOS at each start nor the first start's copy line and bar. The splash had the Pixel 2's pictures, 480x640, and it plays only pictures of the panel's own size. Sylver's fix, in #461, turns them back to 640x480; a card with it is not tried yet |
| Rumble | not tried |
| Wi-Fi | not tried (none built in) |

So the R36S's card boots on the Pixel 2's U-Boot, its root is found, and
the key codes from ROCKNIX's tree are right. His logs say more: the panel is
640x480 on KMSDRM; the joypad (`r36s_Gamepad`, ROCKNIX's
`rocknix-singleadc-joypad`) reports each stick from -1800 to 1800 with a
flat of 32; the battery started at 63% of 3380 mAh; and Diatom's port warns
"could not set every clock ceiling". Other owners say boards differ more
than panels: R36S-V21, an R36XX with Wi-Fi soldered on and no OTG port,
some with 512 MB of RAM.

## Which R36S

The one modelled is the R36S most sources show: 80 x 119 x 35 mm (the
maker's product sheet), a 3.5 in 640x480 IPS panel, 1 GB of DDR3L, a
3500 mAh battery (the device tree says 3380 to 3400 mAh), two microSD slots,
a DC USB-C port, an OTG USB-C port and a headphone jack.

Its face, top to bottom: the glass, from the top edge; the d-pad on the
left and A B X Y on the right (X top, A right, B bottom, Y left), the
speaker's grille between them; FN in the middle; SELECT and START under it;
the two sticks at the bottom. On the left side: POWER, the reset hole and
the TF2-GAME slot. On the right side: the TF1-OS slot and the volume rocker,
VOL+ towards the top. L1, L2, R2 and R1 are tall paddles on the back,
standing on the top of the grip (50 mm under the top edge) up to 38 mm under
it, L1 and R1 outside. At the bottom: DC, the jack and OTG. The thin part is
about 18.8 mm deep, the grip 29.7 mm in all.

Sources for the layout: TechTactician's hands-on review
(https://techtactician.com/r36s-handheld-console-hands-on-review), whose
photos the key places in `r36s_model.c` were measured on (the unboxing photo
straight on, 8 px a mm, which also gives 1.50 for height over width, as 119
over 80 does; the side and bottom photos), iFixit's R36S teardown
(https://www.ifixit.com/Teardown/R36S+Teardown/220069), whose straight photo
of the back gives the shoulders (a rebranded unit in the same shell), and
retrocatalog (https://retrocatalog.com/retro-handhelds/r36s).

Two product pictures do not match it:

- A white R36S with A B X Y on the left and the d-pad on the right is a
  mirrored render: its A is on the left of the diamond and its Y on the
  right, the mirror of the real layout, and every photo of a real unit has
  the d-pad on the left.
- A clear R36S-like handheld of 98 x 166 x 23 mm, with POWER and an "R"
  button on the right side, is another model. I could not name it.

### Clones

"R36S" units are sold with other boards: a different panel, sometimes 512 MB
of RAM, eMMC or Wi-Fi (rocknix.org's page on them:
https://rocknix.org/devices/unbranded/EE-clones/). A clone's stock card has
`rk3326-evb-lp3-v12-linux.dtb` where a genuine one has
`rk3326-r35s-linux.dtb`. dArkOS's README lists the board names printed on
genuine boards (R36S-V12, V20, V21, V22, V30, Y02) and on clones (G80C-MB,
G80CA-MB, G80D-MB, GR36S-MB, ...):
https://github.com/southoz/dArkOSRE-R36/blob/3f372c85aa53a26b265c3dc0c2661ec0fae5faf0/README.md#L29-L68.
This start is for the genuine board only.

## The hardware

The device tree is ROCKNIX's, from its 20260901 release, the release the
Pixel 2's kernel patches come from, in `plastron/buildroot/board/r36s/dts/`
(GPL-2.0+ OR MIT, (c) JELOS). Links below are to ROCKNIX's tree at that tag:
`RK/` is
`https://github.com/ROCKNIX/distribution/blob/20260901/projects/ROCKNIX/devices/RK3326/`.

| What | The R36S | The GKD Pixel 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SoC | RK3326 (`rockchip,rk3326`), not the PX30S | RK3326S, which Linux calls PX30S |
| Panel | 640x480 as it scans, not turned; ST7703, `gameconsole,r36s-panel` (r3xs.dtsi), or another panel through `rocknix,generic-dsi` and an overlay | 480x640, turned 270, generic-dsi |
| Gamepad | `rocknix-singleadc-joypad`, `r36s_Gamepad`, the sticks through a 4-way analog mux on SARADC channel 1 | the same driver, no sticks (`amux-count = <0>`) |
| A B X Y | BTN_EAST, BTN_SOUTH, BTN_NORTH, BTN_WEST | BTN_SOUTH, BTN_EAST, BTN_NORTH, BTN_WEST |
| FN | BTN_MODE | BTN_TRIGGER_HAPPY1 |
| Volume | `gpio-keys`, VOL+ gpio2 PA0, VOL- gpio2 PA1 | `gpio-keys`, other pins |
| Power | the RK817's power key (`rk805-pwrkey`) | the same |
| Rumble | none | a motor on PWM0 |
| Lights | one blue LED, gpio0 PC1 | four battery lights |
| Battery | 3380 mAh, 4.2 V (r3xs.dtsi) | 1730 mAh, 4.35 V |
| Sound | the RK817 codec, `rk817_int`, jack on gpio2 PC6 | the same |
| microSD | two: `sdio` (alias mmc0, card detect gpio3 PB6) and `sdmmc` (mmc1, gpio0 PA3) | one |
| Radio | none (rocknix.org: "Connectivity: None") | none |

The buttons as ROCKNIX's tree has them (`RK/linux/dts/rockchip/rk3326-gameconsole-r36s.dts`),
every one active low with a pull-up, and what the launcher makes of each:

| Key | GPIO | Code | Launcher |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| UP DOWN LEFT RIGHT | gpio1 PB4 PB5 PB6 PB7 | BTN_DPAD_* | the d-pad |
| A | gpio1 PA2 | BTN_EAST | IN_ACCEPT |
| B | gpio1 PA5 | BTN_SOUTH | IN_BACK |
| Y | gpio1 PA6 | BTN_WEST | IN_Y |
| X | gpio1 PA7 | BTN_NORTH | IN_X |
| L3, R3 (the sticks' clicks) | gpio2 PA2, PA3 | BTN_THUMBL, BTN_THUMBR | IN_L3, IN_R3 (named and drawn; no screen acts on them); in a game, the core's L3 and R3 |
| FN | gpio2 PA4 | BTN_MODE | IN_MENU |
| L1, R1 | gpio2 PA6, PA7 | BTN_TL, BTN_TR | IN_L1, IN_R1 |
| L2, R2 | gpio3 PB2, PB7 | BTN_TL2, BTN_TR2 | IN_L2, IN_R2 (plain keys: no battery lights) |
| SELECT, START | gpio3 PB1, PB4 | BTN_SELECT, BTN_START | IN_SELECT, IN_START |
| VOL+, VOL- | gpio2 PA0, PA1 | KEY_VOLUMEUP, KEY_VOLUMEDOWN | IN_VOLUP, IN_VOLDN; with FN, brightness |
| POWER | the RK817 | KEY_POWER | IN_POWER |
| Reset | none in any tree | | probably a hardware reset; untested |

The sticks: analog mux pins gpio3 PB3 (A), PB0 (B), PB5 (enable), both axes
inverted (`invert-absx`, `invert-absy`).

Mainline Linux has no R36S tree. Its closest relative is the ODROID-GO
Super, `rk3326-odroid-go3.dts`, with the same mux pins and the same SELECT,
START, L2 and R2 pins
(https://github.com/torvalds/linux/blob/v7.1/arch/arm64/boot/dts/rockchip/rk3326-odroid-go3.dts),
and the R36S's own vendor tree calls itself `rockchip,rk3326-odroidgo3-linux`.
A mainline-style R36S tree (`io-channel-mux` and `adc-joystick` for the
sticks) is in https://github.com/AndreRenaud/linux-r36s (Linux 6.10,
`arch/arm64/boot/dts/rockchip/rk3326-r36s.dts`, GPL-2.0+ OR MIT), with a
Buildroot port beside it (https://github.com/AndreRenaud/buildroot-r36s).
Beware: that tree gives the same `gameconsole,r36s-panel` name to an NV3051D
panel, where ROCKNIX's means its ST7703.

### Panels

R36S units ship with several panels, and a kernel that drives the wrong one
shows nothing. What is known, from ROCKNIX's tree and the stock device trees
collected in https://github.com/AeolusUX/R36S-DTB (CC BY-NC-ND 4.0: read for
facts only, nothing copied from it):

| Panel | Controller, from its init sequence | How ROCKNIX drives it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0, the original | ST7703 (`B9 F1 12 83`) | built in: `gameconsole,r36s-panel` (panel patch `r36s_desc`, 640x480, 50 MHz) |
| 1 | unknown (`E0 AB BA`) | an overlay |
| 2, 3, 4, 4-V22 | NV3051D (its page unlock, `FF 30 / FF 52 / FF 01`) | an overlay |
| 5 | ST7703, other timings | an overlay |

ROCKNIX's way: one dtb with panel 0, and for the others an overlay on the
boot partition, `overlays/mipi-panel.dtbo`, which U-Boot applies
(`RK/packages/u-boot/config/extlinux/extlinux.conf`, FDTOVERLAYS) and which
sets `compatible = "rocknix,generic-dsi"` and the panel's
`panel_description` (https://rocknix.org/devices/unbranded/game-console-r35s-r36s/).
The generic-dsi driver is already in the Pixel 2's kernel (plastron's patch
`4-rocknix-panel-generic-dsi.patch`). ROCKNIX's overlays are release files of
https://github.com/stolen/r.nix-distribution/releases/tag/panel_overlays,
with no license stated: make ours from the panels' init sequences, not from
those files. To tell a unit's panel: read the stock card's dtb (`dtc -I dtb`)
and compare its `panel-init-sequence` with the table.

#### Picking a panel on the card

The card's boot partition (`R36SBOOT`) holds an overlay for each known
panel, `overlays/panel-0.dtbo` to `panel-4.dtbo` and `panel-4v22.dtbo`
(`plastron/buildroot/board/r36s/overlays/`, whose README says which stock
tree each comes from). Without a choice the card drives panel 0, the first
R36S's. If the screen stays black:

1. Put the card in a computer and open `r36s-boot.txt` on `R36SBOOT`.
2. Find the line `#zy_panel=4`. Take the `#` off and put the panel's name
   after the `=`: `zy_panel=4` (it lit board R36S-V12, Sylver's).
3. Put the card back and start the R36S. If the screen stays black, try the
   next name: 4, 4v22, 2, 3, 1, then 0.

A panel 5 (an ST7703 with other timings) has no overlay yet: no stock tree
for it was found under a license that lets ZyperOS copy it. For it, or a
panel that none of these lights, make the unit's own, as below.

#### A unit's own panel

The first test card lit only after a switch to the device tree for panel 4
(Sylver's, board R36S-V12). The way that needs no one else's file:

1. From the card the R36S came with, take its device tree from the boot
   partition: `rk3326-r35s-linux.dtb` on a genuine board,
   `rk3326-evb-lp3-v12-linux.dtb` on a clone, or whatever `.dtb` its
   `boot.ini` loads.
2. On a computer with `dtc`:

       python3 plastron/scripts/r36s-panel.py rk3326-r35s-linux.dtb mipi-panel.dtbo

   It reads the panel's init sequence, its mode nearest 60 Hz, its size and
   its DSI settings, and writes an overlay that puts them on ZyperOS's
   panel for the generic-dsi driver, with the power-up waits that lit
   board R36S-V12's panel. (The card's own overlays are made this way.)
3. Copy `mipi-panel.dtbo` to the R36S card's boot partition (`R36SBOOT`) as
   `overlays/mipi-panel.dtbo`. The boot script lays it over the device tree
   at every start.

Or copy it as `overlays/panel-mine.dtbo` and write `zy_panel=mine` in
`r36s-boot.txt`.

#### A plan for choosing it without a computer

In order of how much each needs:

1. **At the first start, from the stock tree on the card.** If the boot
   partition holds the unit's own stock `.dtb` (the installer, or the
   player, puts it there), the first start converts it as
   `r36s-panel.py` does, writes `overlays/mipi-panel.dtbo` and restarts. The
   image needs `dtc` (Buildroot's `dtc` package with its programs) or a small
   overlay writer of its own. The panel stays dark until that restart, so it
   runs before anything is shown.
2. **At power-on, by a held key.** U-Boot can read the keys (`gpio input`,
   which plastron's U-Boot has, `CONFIG_CMD_GPIO`): a key held at power-on
   picks `overlays/panel-N.dtbo` and keeps the choice in `r36s-boot.txt`, as
   ArkOS's R3XS build picks its panels with R1 and a direction. Needs the
   GPIO names checked on a unit, and the overlays on the card first.
3. **By the panel's own ID.** A MIPI panel answers DCS reads of its ID
   (0xDA to 0xDC) after reset, before any init sequence. The generic-dsi
   driver already sends reads (`I read=`); a kernel change could read the ID
   first and pick among several panels' descriptions in one device tree, so
   every R36S boots the same card. Needs each panel's ID read on a unit:
   `I read=` lines in an overlay print them in `dmesg`.

### The bootloader

ROCKNIX boots the R36S on its legacy Hardkernel U-Boot (`RK/packages/u-boot-legacy/`),
with Rockchip's `rk3326_ddr_333MHz_v2.12.bin`, `rk3326_miniloader_v1.40.bin`
and `rk3326_bl31_v1.37.elf`, the same rkbin files as the Pixel 2's. A
mainline U-Boot for it: https://github.com/AndreRenaud/u-boot-r36s
(v2024.04, `configs/r36s_defconfig`, GPL-2.0+).

The Pixel 2's U-Boot (`plastron/buildroot/board/px2/u-boot/`) has a patch
for the PX30S's DDR frequencies (`0004-px2-px30s-dmc-fsp.patch`). It may not
fit a plain RK3326, so do not put the Pixel 2's U-Boot on an R36S card
without reading it first.

## Building the kernel

    make -C plastron r36s-kernel

Needs Docker and the Pixel 2's image built once before (`make -C plastron`),
which downloads the Linux source this reuses. It applies the Pixel 2's kernel
patches, merges `board/r36s/linux-r36s.fragment` into the Pixel 2's config,
and builds `Image` and `rk3326-gameconsole-r36s.dtb` with four jobs (`JOBS=N`
for more) into `plastron/out/r36s-kernel/`. It stops if a line of the
fragment does not hold.

## A test card

### Building it

    make -C plastron r36s DEV_KEYS=

Needs Docker; the first build takes hours (the whole toolchain), on four
CPUs (`R36S_CPUS`). It is the Pixel 2's system (`configs/r36s_defconfig`,
`FLAVOR=r36s`) with the R36S's kernel and device trees, its launcher
(`src/device/r36s.c`, `DEVICE=r36s`) and Diatom port (`diatom/port/r36s.c`),
and writes `plastron/out/r36s-sdcard.img`. `DEV_KEYS=` leaves out the SSH key
a development image otherwise takes from `plastron/local/authorized_keys`:
put yours there before building to reach the R36S over SSH.

### Flashing it

Write `r36s-sdcard.img` to a microSD card with any program that writes disk
images (balenaEtcher, Raspberry Pi Imager, `dd`). It erases the whole card.
Put it in the slot the R36S starts from, **TF1-OS** (the right side), with
nothing in TF2. The first start adds the games partition over the rest of
the card and copies ZyperOS onto it, which takes a few minutes.

### What it does differently from a release

- The kernel writes its messages on the screen as it starts (DRM's log
  client) and on the serial port (`ttyS2`, 1500000 baud), so a start that
  stops shows where.
- `r36s-boot.txt` on the boot partition (`R36SBOOT`) changes the start
  without building anything: another device tree (ROCKNIX's own is on the
  card too), a panel overlay, other kernel arguments. Each line in it says
  what it does.
- A panel overlay at `overlays/mipi-panel.dtbo` on the boot partition,
  ROCKNIX's file for the R36S's other panels, is laid over the device tree
  when it is there: a unit whose screen stays dark or wrong with the first
  panel's driver can try its panel's overlay made as
  https://rocknix.org/devices/unbranded/game-console-r35s-r36s/ says.
- Its log says what a report needs: the device tree that booted, the
  kernel's lines about the panel and the cards, and the input devices
  (`sd/r36s/device.sh`, `device_log`).

### What can only be decided on the unit, and how to switch

| Question | The test card's answer | To try another |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The panel | the ST7703 of the first R36S panel | `zy_panel=N` in `r36s-boot.txt` for the card's `overlays/panel-N.dtbo` (0 to 4, 4v22), or `overlays/mipi-panel.dtbo` on the boot partition |
| The bootloader | the Pixel 2's U-Boot (ROCKNIX's RK3326 U-Boot); its PX30S-only part does nothing on an RK3326 | another `u-boot` image at the card's 32 KiB (`board/r36s/genimage.cfg`) |
| Which card is mmcblk0 | the boot card, by ZyperOS's tree (`rk3326-gameconsole-r36s-zyperos.dts` swaps ROCKNIX's aliases): the RK3326 boots from its sdmmc controller only | `zy_dtb=rk3326-gameconsole-r36s.dtb` in `r36s-boot.txt` gives ROCKNIX's order |
| The key codes | ROCKNIX's tree: A BTN_EAST, B BTN_SOUTH, FN BTN_MODE | `RK_CODE_*` in `src/device/r36s.c` and `diatom/port/r36s.c` |

### Logs back

- On the card: `zyperos-setup.txt` on the boot partition (the first start,
  written as it goes), and `.userdata/r36s/logs/` on the games partition
  (each start's `zyperos.log`).
- With File Transfer, once the screen and the keys work: **Download logs**
  on its page (Wi-Fi needs a USB adapter on the OTG port).

## For a tester with an R36S

What answers most of the questions below, from the firmware the R36S runs
now (ArkOS, dArkOS, ROCKNIX or its stock one). Nothing in it writes to the
device.

### 1. Which R36S it is

- The board's name and version, printed on the board if you open it
  (for example "R36S-V20 2024-..." or "G80CA-MB"), and a photo of it.
- The dtb name on the stock or the current card's boot partition
  (`rk3326-r35s-linux.dtb`, `rk3326-evb-lp3-v12-linux.dtb`, or another), and
  a copy of that file.

### 2. From its current firmware, over SSH or a terminal

```sh
cat /proc/device-tree/model
cat /proc/device-tree/compatible | tr '\0' '\n'
uname -a
ls -l /dev/mmcblk*
cat /proc/cmdline
dmesg | grep -iE 'panel|dsi|st7703|nv3051|generic-dsi|mmc'
cat /sys/class/power_supply/*/uevent
```

And the full `dmesg` as a file.

Then `evtest` (install it if it is missing):

- On the gamepad: press every key once, in this order: UP, DOWN, LEFT, RIGHT,
  A, B, X, Y, L1, L2, R1, R2, SELECT, START, FN, L3 (left stick click), R3
  (right stick click).
- Then move each stick slowly to its four ends, and let it go back to the
  middle.
- On the volume device: VOL+ and VOL-.
- On the power key: POWER.
- And press the reset hole once with a pin, to see if it is a key at all.

Please save each device's output to a file.

### 3. Measurements with calipers (mm)

- The body: width, height, and depth at the front part (above the grip) and
  at the grip.
- The corner radius at the top and at the bottom of the front.
- The glass: width, height, and its distance from the top and from the left
  edge.
- The lit screen area: width and height (with the screen on, white).
- The d-pad: its centre (from the top and the left edge), its total span and
  the width of one arm.
- A, B, X, Y, FN, SELECT and START: each key's diameter and centre (from the
  top and the left edge).
- The sticks: the cap's diameter, the well's diameter, each centre, and how
  far the cap stands above the face.
- POWER and the reset hole (left side), the volume rocker (right side): their
  lengths, and their centres from the top edge.
- The shoulders: each one's width and height, and where they start and end,
  from the top edge and from the side.
- Where the grip starts, from the top edge.
- The ports at the bottom (DC, the jack, OTG): their centres from the left
  edge.
- The two card slots: their centres from the top edge.

Straight on, from as far as the camera allows, zoomed in, so the picture is
not distorted:

- the front (screen off), the back, the left side, the right side, the top,
  the bottom.

### 5. On a ZyperOS test card

The test card (above, "A test card") sends its logs back two ways:

- On the card itself: `zyperos-setup.txt` on the boot partition (what the
  first start did, written as it goes, so a first start that hangs can still
  be read on a computer), and `.userdata/r36s/logs/` on the games partition
  (each start's `zyperos.log`).
- Or with File Transfer: **MENU > Wi-Fi File Transfer** (or USB File
  Transfer over the cable), open its address in a browser, and click
  **Download logs**. That needs the screen and the keys to work.

## What only a real R36S can answer

- Which panel each board has, and how to tell them apart without a tester
  trying device trees (board R36S-V12, Sylver's, has panel 4).
- Whether the Pixel 2's U-Boot starts every board (it started R36S-V12).
- The sticks' centre on other boards (R36S-V12's read -1800 to 1800, flat
  32), and whether the reset hole is a key at all.
- The battery's real cutoff and how the RK817 reads it.
- Whether the OTG port works as a USB device.
- The speaker's level and whether headphone detection is active high, as
  ROCKNIX's tree says, or low, as the stock tree says.

The list above, "For a tester with an R36S", is how to find out.
