# ZyperOS design

This is the design system for everything ZyperOS puts on a screen: the shelf,
the menus, Muse, the keyboard, the volume bar over a game or a port, the
notices Diatom shows, the loading screen, the tutorial, and the sounds they
make. A screen that is not described here is described by the nearest thing
that is, and then it is added here.

Every value in this document has a name, and the code uses the name. The
tables marked *generated* are written by `tools/designdef.py`, which is the
one place a value is typed; `make design` writes them here, into
`src/design.h` for the launcher, Diatom's overlay and portkeys, and into
`docs/design/mock/tokens.js` for the mock kit. `make check` fails when any of
those disagree, and when a text color falls under the contrast it is owed.
To change a value, change `tools/designdef.py`, run `make design`, and say
why in the commit.

The choices here were made by me on 2026-11-04 in the design lab
(`docs/design/mock/`), from options drawn on live screens: two themes,
**Beard** (light, the default) and **Salt & Pepper** (dark); focus as an
accent fill; Lexend; soft corners; snappy motion; Zyper as a companion; soft
interface sounds; and a full reward in the tutorial. Where this document
settles something the lab did not ask about, it says so and gives the reason.

---

## 1. Principles

1. **The games are the content; the system is the frame.** Color, light and
   motion belong first to the shelf of covers. The chrome around them is
   quiet greys and one accent, so a cover never competes with a button.
2. **Every press is answered in the next frame.** A move moves, a press
   presses, a dead row says why it is dead. Nothing waits in silence, and a
   press nobody can act on still gets a sound and a reason.
3. **One meaning per color.** Lime is ZyperOS: the focus, the chrome that is
   the system's, success. A system's color is that system's. Warm is light,
   pink is love, red-orange is a warning. A color is never borrowed for
   decoration.
4. **Motion says where something came from and where it went.** It is
   short, it starts from wherever the thing is now, a new press redirects it
   rather than queuing behind it, and it never makes anyone wait for it.
5. **Built for a 640x480 panel at arm's length.** Everything is designed in
   the launcher's 1024x768 space and shown at 0.625. Text is never smaller
   than 37 units (23 panel pixels) and always meets its contrast; marks meet
   theirs.
6. **Say it plainly.** Short words, the button's own name, the state in the
   value column rather than a row that vanishes (src/sys_menu.c says why).
7. **Zyper is the warmth, and he is rationed.** He is at the moments that
   matter and in the corner of the menus, never in the way of a game.
8. **Nothing is cut off.** Truncation destroys information (src/ui.h); text
   that does not fit scrolls.

## 3. Color

### 3.1 Themes

**Beard** is the light theme and the default for a new device: the white of
Zyper's beard as the ground, charcoal text, lime as a fill with dark text on
it. **Salt & Pepper** is the dark theme: Zyper's coat as the whole UI, the
charcoal of his nose and eyes as the ground (the same 17,19,16 the power-off
screen has always cleared to, so the two grounds of ZyperOS become one), the
dark cap for surfaces, beard white for text.

MENU > Appearance chooses: **Light**, **Dark**, or **Auto**, which is Light
from 7:00 to 19:00 by the handheld's clock. *(The setting's name and Auto's
hours are this document's call.)* The shelf has one card style and one horizontal layout.

### 3.2 Roles

Each role has one value per theme. Code asks for the role, never the value.

- **ground** clears the screen. **surface** is a panel, a card's slab, the

  (src/ui.h). Unselected live rows are **text-soft**, not dimmed to

  tutorial's check. Text on it is **on-accent**.

  volume, an earned achievement, the progress strip. In Beard it is a darker
  lime, because the fill's lime on a light ground is only 1.3:1.
- **light** is brightness, **favorite** the heart, **warning** low battery
  and errors.
- **store-games**, **store-saves** and **store-art** are Storage's bar and
  its legend's dots (11.32), and nothing else.

### 3.3 Accents

| Accent | Is | Never |
|---|---|---|
| lime (**accent**, **accent-ink**) | ZyperOS: focus, the system's chrome, volume, success, earned, Muse | a system's color, decoration |
| a system's color (systems.cfg) | that system's menu heading rule and missing-cover fallback | focus, source sprite colors, unrelated screens |
| **light** | brightness | anything else |
| **favorite** | the heart | anything else |
| **warning** | low battery, an error a row reports | emphasis |

*This document's calls, from the audit (2026-11-04):* Muse is no longer its
own green (0x9CD345 sat a few degrees from Zyper's lime and the two could not
be told apart); it uses lime like the rest of the system. Volume is lime in
both themes (Salt & Pepper is one-accent), and an earned achievement is lime
where it was cyan. Cyan leaves the system.

A system's color on the Beard ground is mixed a quarter of the way toward
**text** before it is used as a mark (a rail, a heading rule), so a yellow
like Game Boy Color's still reads on a light ground. Source console sprites
and game covers are never recolored for a theme. Their dark outlines remain
opaque. Focus uses the accent underline, size and text, not a tint of the art.

### 3.4 Scrims

| Scrim | Dark | Light | Under |
|---|---|---|---|
| **scrim.menu** | ink at 47% | ink at 28% | every menu, keyboard and one-line message over the shelf |
| **scrim.game** | ink at 73% | ink at 73% | the paused game under the in-game menu, save slots, achievements |

Two levels, not four. The game keeps the heavy one in both themes because a
game is content, not part of the theme. *(The audit found 120, 150 and 185
for these; the keyboard's 150 becomes **scrim.menu**, and the in-game menu,
which used the shelf's 120 over a paused game, gets **scrim.game** like the
slots beside it.)* "Could not start" and "Connect to Wi-Fi first." panels, which
drew with no scrim at all, get **scrim.menu**.

## 4. Type

Lexend, the whole UI, in two weights: **Regular** for everything, and
**SemiBold** for the name under the cursor (the **title** role). Lexend was
drawn for reading speed; at the same size as the Josefin Sans it replaces it
sets the launcher's words about 8% wider (4 to 19% over 54 of its strings,
measured 2026-11-04) and its x-height is a quarter taller, so it reads larger
at the panel's 23 pixels.

The two weights are two files in one folder, `menu.ttf` (Regular) and
`title.ttf` (SemiBold). Regular keeps the name it always had, so plastron
picks it up without a change; SemiBold is found beside it, and Wi-Fi File Transfer's
page loads both. `menu.ttf` serves the UI, the in-game menu, the titles the
launcher draws into the cards it generates (src/ui.c) and tools/gensingles.py;
`title.ttf` serves the title role, Wi-Fi File Transfer's bold and the names on
the home and games screens. A card without `title.ttf` draws the
title in Regular, and the launcher says so once on stderr. Both files are
under the SIL Open Font License (`res/fonts/OFL.txt`).

Lexend has no Chinese or Japanese. In **简体中文** and **日本語** (11.30)
every role is drawn in **Noto Sans SC** or **Noto Sans JP**, Regular for
all but the title and Bold for the title (their static cuts have no
SemiBold), `zh-menu.otf`, `zh-title.otf`, `ja-menu.otf` and `ja-title.otf`
beside `menu.ttf`: tools/cjkfont.py's cuts to the first level of GB 2312 or
of JIS X 0208's kanji, the kana, and every character ZyperOS writes, 700 to
830 KB a weight (`res/fonts/OFL-NotoSansSC.txt`, `OFL-NotoSansJP.txt`). Two
faces, since the two write some of the same characters differently. A set
opens the first time its language or text is drawn and stays open; nothing
else changes. A line with characters its face lacks is drawn in the first of
its role's faces that has them all (the set that is on, then Japanese's for
kana, Chinese's otherwise), placed by the capitals of the face it was asked
in (src/ui.c, `face_for`), so 简体中文 and 日本語 read in an English menu and
a game named in either is not boxes. The button letters
and the cards the launcher draws stay Lexend in every language: they are
marks. Measured on an M3 against Lexend (2026-11-07): 1 ms to open the eight
sizes, 0.5 MB more memory, and a new line of Chinese about six times
Lexend's time the first time it is drawn, then cached like any line.

- Text is placed by its capitals, not its em box: centered on them in a row,
  a key or a band (`ui_cap_y`), and a line given the top of its line has them
  two fifths of an em down (`ui_line_y`). A role's line is its size, an em
  (`ui_font_line`), not the face's line skip: Lexend's ascent is a whole em,
  a quarter of it room for accents, and a menu row of 1.5 skips would be 93
  where the mock's is 77.
- Digits that change while on screen are tabular (`ui_text_tabular`).
- **Title Case** for headings, menu rows and named choices ("Audio Output",
  "Light"). **lowercase** for status words in the value column
  ("off", "not connected", "needs Wi-Fi"). **Sentence case** for lines and
  messages ("Press A to open the game you are on.").
- No italics, no letter spacing, no all-caps except button names, which are
  written as printed on the handheld: A, B, X, Y, L1, R1, L2, R2, START,
  SELECT, MENU, VOL+, VOL-, POWER.

## 5. Space and layout

- Positions and sizes are multiples of 8 units where a value is new; the
  values below that are not (the rail's 23) are kept for the reason in the
  code beside them.
- The menu panel is laid out from one number, `row_h` = 1.5 x the menu
  line, and everything else is a fraction of it (padding 0.75, label-to-value
  gap 1, heading `line(label) + pad`), so the panel scales as a whole.
- The standard panel is 800 units wide, centered. Content is never wider than
  the screen less two insets and two paddings.
- The rail is 6 units, 23 from the bottom (or left) edge, inset 90 at both
  ends; its marker is at least 18.

## 7. Elevation

Salt & Pepper lifts with light: the additive glow under a focused card, in
the system's color, as the launcher has always done. Beard lifts with
shadow: light on a light ground is invisible, so the same places get a soft
shadow of ink instead, neutral, never tinted. In Beard the system's color is
the wash along the bottom of the shelf (a normal blend, not additive), the
card's band and the rail.

## 8. Motion

**Snappy.** Fast and sharp, like hardware: things arrive on a steep ease-out
and are there before the thumb is off the button. Nothing overshoots in the
interface. Zyper alone moves like a character (8.3).

### 8.1 Curves

| Curve | Formula | For |
|---|---|---|
| **out** | `1 - (1-t)^5` (quintic ease-out) | everything that arrives or moves: a card step, a panel opening, the focus fill, the bar coming in, a check appearing |
| **in** | `t^3` (cubic ease-in) | things leaving: a panel closing, the launch zoom |
| **turn** | `t^2 (3 - 2t)` (smoothstep) | the tutorial details panel |
| **fade** | linear | opacity of something going away; marquees |
| **follow** | `1 - e^(-dt/tau)` | tracking a moving target every frame: the background tint, the scrolling window |

*(The audit found cubic ease-out written four times and a quadratic ease-in
beside a cubic one. One function each, in `src/design.h`.)*

- **Every motion is interruptible.** A new press retargets from wherever
  the thing is now; nothing queues.
- **Every motion tells the redraw clock when it ends**, the way
  `plat_osd_until` does, so a screen that redraws only on change still draws
  the last frame.
- **Menus now open and close.** A panel opens in **move** (out): from 96% to
  full size and from clear to opaque, with its scrim fading in over the same
  time; it closes in **exit** (in), back the same way. The focus fill glides
  between rows in **quick** (out). *(New; menus snapped open before.)*
- **One panel at a time.** Going deeper (MENU > Wi-Fi > a network > the
  keyboard) the parent leaves in **exit** while the child opens in **move**,
  starting together, and the scrim stays as it is; going back is the same
  the other way. Only the first panel fades the scrim in, and only the last
  fades it out. *(This document's call: the launcher's menus are modal
  loops, each drawing the shelf, a scrim and one panel, and a stack of live
  panels would make every level wait on the ones under it.)*
- **One-line messages** ("Saved", "Could not start") hold for **hold.short**;
  two-line ones for **hold.long**. *(The audit found five hold times, 900 to
  2200 ms.)*

### 8.3 Zyper's motion

Zyper is drawn from triangles (src/zypermark.h) and has six moves, from the
boot animation and the loading loop; their times are the **zyper-** tokens of
8.2:

| Move | What | Time |
|---|---|---|
| hop | up 24 units and back, squashing 6% at the bottom | 280 ms |
| tilt | the head cocks 10 degrees, the high brow lifts | 160 ms |
| tongue | out or in | 160 ms |
| pant | the tongue 18% in and out, three times a second | 1 s |
| blink | the eyes shut and open again | 134 ms |
| breathe | at rest, a 2-unit bob | 2.4 s loop |

He may overshoot and settle; the interface around him does not. A move he
is already making is not started over: a hop under way runs on, and a pant
goes on to the end of its beat, so a held key that changes a setting every
90 ms does not drop him mid-air.

## 9. Sound

**Soft UI.** Short synthesized sounds, quiet, from the launcher only: never
over a game or a port. One exception: the sound of the press that starts or
resumes a game, A's accept or Continue's back, is let finish, at most about
100 ms over the game's first frames; cut, every launch's tick would be
clipped. They play through the system volume, so the volume keys and the
mute switch set them too.

They play on the speaker, the headphone jack and a USB-C DAC, through the
same shared output Muse and Diatom use, in every screen the launcher draws,
the in-game menu over a paused game included. With a Bluetooth headset as
the output they are off: its A2DP stream is held by one player at a time,
and its fifth of a second of delay would put every tick after the press it
answers. *(This document's call.)* They follow Audio Channels: mono on one
side plays them on that side only.

There are three **voices** of the same six sounds, each sound a WAV that
`tools/gensounds.py` synthesizes from the tones below (`make sounds`): Wood,
soft mallets on wooden bars; Glass, pure high ticks; Bubble, drops that pop.
**MENU > UI Sounds**, under Audio Channels, picks Wood, Glass, Bubble or Off,
and a new device has **Wood**. The row plays each voice as it is chosen.
*(2026-11-04, in the sound lab, docs/design/mock/sound-lab.html:
Wood, with the voice a setting rather than one fixed set; the row with the
audio rows.)*

- A step that moves plays **move**. A step that cannot, at the end of a list,
  plays **edge**, once a press: held against the end, the repeats are
  silent.
- A menu list does not wrap (11.3), so a step down from the last row or up
  from the first plays **edge**. A shelf or the home that wraps from the last
  item to the first plays **move**. A held wrap uses the normal held level. A
  blocked edge still plays **edge**.
- A held direction repeats every 90 ms after 300 ms. Its first step plays at
  full level and every repeat at half (**held**, 6 dB down), so the shelf is
  heard running and a long hold does not fill the room. The edge a hold comes
  to is news, and plays at full level. *(2026-11-04, in the sound lab,
  the held level.)*
- Left and right on a menu row step its value. A value that steps round
  (Appearance, Sort By, the in-game menu's Display) plays **move** on the

  plays **move** when it changed and **edge** when it did not.

  in-game menu); A on a row that does nothing with it (Auto Off, Sort By, a
  system's Display Mode) plays **deny**. B plays **back**, and so does MENU
  closing a menu.
  A menu the launcher opens itself, or a game it starts with nobody
  pressing (a resume at boot), plays nothing.
- A press on a dead row, or of a button the screen has nothing for where it
  usually acts (X or Y on Muse's albums), plays **deny**, and the row says
  why.
- **reward** is for something good: a tutorial step, a favorite added.
  Taking a favorite away plays **back**.
- On the keyboard A types and plays **accept**, B deletes and plays
  **back**; the grid wraps at every edge, and its wrap plays **move**.

### 11.5 Keyboard
The whole keyboard of src/keyboard.c, every decision in it kept because each
saves presses on a D-pad: four rows of ten keys with the digits on every
layer, three layers (letters, capitals, symbols), the grid wrapping at every
edge; the face buttons are verbs - A types, B deletes, X shifts, Y goes to
the symbols - L1/R1 move the cursor, START is done, MENU cancels; the text is
never hidden.

Under the grid, an **action row** of keys that do the same as the buttons:
shift, symbols, space, delete, done. Each shows its button's glyph beside its
label (X shift, Y #+=, B delete, START done; space is drawn as the space
mark), and X's and Y's labels follow the layer ("unshift", "abc"). One line
under the row, in **meta** **text-dim**, names the two with no key: L1/R1
cursor, MENU cancel. A has no caption: it is OK everywhere. *(
2026-11-04, in the keyboard lab: the action row, with one quiet line instead
of the two rows of seven captions it had.)*

The panel is 940 wide, so a button and its label fit in each action key at
**meta** size. In Lexend the longest, "unshift", has 3 to 4 units to spare at
each side of its key; a face that sets it wider gives up half the gap between
button and label and then the end of the label, never the key's edge. Keys are
**raised**, **radius.key**; the selected key is the **accent** fill with
**on-accent** text, its glyph outlined in **on-accent**; the field is
**ground** inside an **edge** line, with a 3-unit **accent-ink** caret (lime
ink, since the fill's lime is 1.3:1 on Beard's ground). A press pops the key
in **press** and plays **accept**; a delete plays **back**.

### 11.6 Volume and brightness bar
A pill (src/osdbar.c) 452 x 64 at the top center, 22 below the top edge:
**surface** at 93%, **edge**, **elevation.bar**. The mark - a speaker with one
to three waves and a cross at silence, or a sun whose rays grow - in
**accent-ink** for volume and **light** for brightness, and one segment per

### 11.7 Notice (over a game)
A slab of **surface** at 85%, **radius.panel**, at the bottom center; heading
in **meta** **text-dim**, body in **label** **text**.

tall as the two lines, corners **radius.row**, at the left, and the lines
left-aligned beside it. A progress notice is the achievement's name in

and the count ("74 / 100") in **meta** **text** under it. Notices take turns:
each has its time and a 300 ms gap before the next; unlocks go ahead of
progress. The achievements list (MENU > Achievements) has the badge at the start
of every row, greyed and faded until earned, and the count where the points
are while it moves. *(My picks in the achievements lab,
docs/design/mock/cheevos-lab.html, 2026-11-05: badge at the left, strip, on

**The plain badge.** Where the card has no badge for an achievement, none yet
or never (a handheld with no network has none until a computer puts them
there, `tools/ra-sets.py`), the place is not left empty: a trophy on a tile,
the badge's size and corners, in the theme's own colors (src/noticeart.h).

is missing (src/rabadge.h); no frame waits on the network.

### 11.9 Button glyphs
For the tutorial and anywhere a button is drawn rather than named
(`ui_button`, `ui_dpad`, `ui_rocker`, `ui_power` in src/ui.c). Face buttons
are circles, 96 units, **raised**, a 4-unit **edge** ring, the letter in
**title**. SELECT and START are pills 64 high, the word Regular at half the
title, 30 units at each end. MENU is a face, as the key is on the Pixel 2: a
round key with three dimples down the device's height
(tools/build-pixel2-model.py), so its glyph is the face circle with three
**text** dots in a vertical line, each 0.15 of the face letter's cap height
across in radius and half of it apart, in place of a letter *(
2026-11-05: "don't write menu... do the circle with the three dots")*.
Wherever running text says MENU (a hint, a Controls row, a message, "MENU >
Controls"), `ui_text` draws the same key inline: a ring 1.3 capitals across
in the text's own color, the three dots inside, centered on the capitals,
and `ui_text_width` measures it so fitting and breaking agree. "Menu" in
lower case stays a word. The D-pad is a cross 120 across: arms of the ring
40 wide with a corner of 10, the face inset 4 with a corner of 7, an **edge**
dot in the middle and an arrow per arm in **text**. VOL+ and VOL- are one
rocker pill 64 high with a half each, VOL- on the left, 24 units at each end
of each half's word, and a 2-unit **edge** line between them. POWER is a
96-unit circle with the power mark in **text**: a ring of 20 open at the top,
a stroke of 6 with round ends, and a stroke down into it.

**In a menu row**, a glyph says a button does the row's job from anywhere on
the page: the row's value is `MENU_KEY_X` (src/menu.h), and the glyph is
drawn at 40, the keyboard's action keys' size, where the value's right edge
is, plain off the fill and UI_BUTTON_ON_ACCENT on it. The in-game menu's Quit
is the one (11.28). "MENU, X" names the press on a Controls page: a comma for
one press after the other, as a plus is for held together.

A pressed glyph is 92% size, its ring **accent**, with a soft **accent** halo
(16 units of blur at three quarters), in **press**. A pressed arm of the
D-pad or half of the rocker fills with **accent** and its arrow or word turns
**on-accent**, so the direction shows. A glyph may also be drawn larger about
its middle (the tutorial's pulse after a wrong press) and faded (with the
line it belongs to). At rest it is drawn by the same rule as before, pixel for
pixel.

### 11.26 Charge limit

My picks in the charge limit lab (docs/design/mock/charge-limit-lab.html,
2026-11-06). A charge the battery stops at, to keep it healthy for longer: a
lithium battery ages slower when it is not kept full.

**Charge Limit** in MENU, directly under Auto Off and over Side Lights:
**Off**, the default on every card, a full charge as before; **80%**,
**85%**, **90%**. Left and right step it round, A steps it on, heard as a
ring, as Side Lights is. Only where the charger can stop short: plastron's
rk817 driver on the GKD Pixel 2 (`charge_control_end_threshold`), which at
the limit lets the cable give only 80 mA, less than the handheld draws, so
the battery takes no charge and slowly gives the rest, and 2 points under it
(`charge_control_start_threshold`) charges again: the battery holds between
the limit minus 2 and the limit, 78 to 80% at 80%, it does not sit full all
day on a desk, and it is never off the system (turning the charger off took
it off, and pulling the cable then cut the power, on a Pixel 2). The band was
5 points at first, and 80% fell to 75% before it charged again, which looked
like a limit that slipped (2026-11-07). 2 is safe: the level is the gauge's
coulomb counter, which moves only with the charge, so each switch waits for a
point of charge, minutes, and never flickers on and off. And it is kind to
the battery: held, it gives the handheld the same current whatever the band,
so a narrow band only cuts that into shorter top-ups, and a top-up of a point
near 80% stresses it little. The launcher writes the start as well as the
end, so a system from before the change holds the same band. The kernel
forgets the limit at a power-off, and the launcher sets it at every start.
The percentage stays true: 100% is still a full battery. With the cable out
the charger is on again, so a charger on a handheld that is off, with no
kernel to keep the limit, charges it.

On the charging screen (11.25), with a limit: the time is to the limit, "85%
in about 40 min" ("Working out the time to 85%", "Almost at 85%"); held at
it, the speed line is "Kept at 85% · wall charger", the time line "Charge
Limit in MENU", the battery mark has the plug (11.12), and the hint grows to
"Hold (A) for a full charge · Hold (POWER) to start", the bar under it
filling for whichever is held. A battery charged full before the limit was
set is held at 100%: the screen says "Charged" and "Fully charged" as any
full one, with the plug, and offers no full charge. A held for **hold-unlock** is a full charge,
this charge only: the limit is off until the cable comes out, and on again
then (a file in /tmp, so a power-off ends it too). The corner's battery has
the plug while it is held, everywhere.

### 11.27 Side lights

My picks in the side lights lab (`docs/design/mock/side-lights-lab.html`,
2026-11-06), after he asked whether the lights could be lit one by one for
more modes. The GKD Pixel 2's four lights under POWER, bottom to top: the
bottom one red or green (both at once read as yellow on the device, as I
saw it 2026-11-06), then three green. Each is on
or off, nothing between (GPIO pins, no PWM). plastron's gauge draws them
(package/gauge/src/lights.c, a pure function with host checks); the launcher
only tells it what happened, a line at a time on its FIFO `/run/gauge`
(src/sidelights.h), and never waits. Diatom does nothing for them, so a game
loses no frames. A step of a moving pattern lasts 100 ms ("Even, 100 ms").

- **Charging Bar**, on the cable: the lights held for each full quarter, then
  one more every 400 ms up to all four, and again, in games as on the shelf
  ("Moving, as today"). Still at all four when full, and still at the level
  (at least one light) while Charge Limit holds the battery ("Still at the
  limit"; the battery's status says Not charging); it moves again when
  charging starts again under the limit.
- **Low Battery**, off the cable ("Two stages"): under 10% the bottom light
  flashes red 200 ms every 5 s, under 5% twice (150 ms on, 150 off, 150 on)
  every 2 s. The first flash comes the moment it falls under each, and when
  the cable comes out.
- **Volume and Brightness**, while one changes ("Eight steps, a half
  blinks"): each light is two of eight steps; the one above the lit ones
  blinks (250 ms on, 250 off) for a half. Volume at 0 lights the bottom one
  red; the dimmest brightness is a half, never red. Held as long as the
  screen's bar (**hold-bar**, 1.5 s after the last change). From the volume
  keys on the shelf and in menus, from Diatom's LEVEL lines in a game (a
  move only: the levels Diatom reports as a game starts are not a press), and
  from portkeys over a port.
- **Files and Covers Done** ("Climb twice"): one light runs up the four,
  twice, 0.8 s, when a File Transfer batch is in (files arrived, then none
  for 2 s: the page sends a batch one file at a time) and when a Scraper run
  ends without a problem.
- **Achievements** ("A sparkle"): the bottom and third lights, then the
  second and top, three times each, 150 ms a step, 0.9 s, the moment one
  unlocks in a game, while its pop-up waits for the menu.
- **No game light** ("Not on the lights"): nothing shows that a game runs.

Sharing them ("Newest on top"): a level, a Done or a sparkle takes the lights
for its time, over the charging bar, and the newest replaces the one before;
then the bar comes back. The low-battery red is laid over everything, a
pattern included (it is the bottom light's red, which no pattern uses but
silence), and is hidden only when Low Battery is off. L2+R2 flashes the
battery level over all of it, four times in 2 s, in every setting.

**Side Lights** in MENU, under Charge Limit, says how many parts are on ("All
on", "3 on", "Off"); A opens its page ("A page of switches"): Charging Bar,
Low Battery, Volume and Brightness, Files and Covers Done, Achievements, each
On or Off, stepped by Left, Right or A and heard as a ring. Every change is
written to the card at once and the gauge reads it again, so the lights
follow the row. Everything is on for a new card and for a card that said
Auto ("Everything on"); a card set to Charging, Low Battery or Off before
keeps what it said, the newer parts off. The setting is a line in
`.userdata/pixel2/side-lights`: the old word first (auto, charging, low,
off), which an older gauge reads alone, then `level`/`-level`, `done`/`-done`,
`cheer`/`-cheer`.

### 11.30 Languages

ZyperOS speaks **English**, **简体中文**, **Español**, **Português
(Brasil)**, **Français**, **Deutsch**, **日本語** and **Italiano** *(
2026-11-07: "the most famous 5 languages, including portuguese", his picks
in the languages lab, docs/design/mock/languages-lab.html; then "add german,

### 11.31 Delete Game

A game's details (X on the shelf) end in **Delete Game**, after Cover and
Video (11.34) *(2026-11-07: "when clicking X on a game to see details, allow
deleting that game. delete the game, saves, and media")*. A plain row, as
Cover and Video is: live on every shelf, with no value, since what it
takes is the question's to say. Never where the page opens, and A on it
only asks. A BIOS set on a shelf (`neogeo.zip`) is never deleted: A says so
in a one-line message (11.8), "neogeo is a BIOS other games need. It stays."

**A port** (the Ports shelf) is its script and the folders it runs from,
which its folder's `port.json` names (PortMaster writes it: its "items",
`["stardewvalley/", "StardewValley.sh"]`), or, with none, the folder its
script's `GAMEDIR` names (src/portinfo.h). Its question says so, "Delete
StardewValley? Its folders go too, its saves with them.", since a port
keeps its saves in them. A folder another port on the shelf names stays.
PortMaster itself, which every port runs on, never goes: "PortMaster runs
every port. It stays."; nor does a port nothing names a folder for:
"Nothing says which folders are Mystery's. It stays." Both are said before
anything is asked. *(2026-11-08, from Tarik's Pixel 2: Storage showed each
port as its script alone, and deleting one from there left its folders.
Ports had had no Delete Game, since nothing had said what was a port's.)*

- **The question** is the confirm panel every destructive row asks with
  (Forget, Unlink, Delete All, Roll Back): over the shelf on **scrim.menu**,
  heading **Delete Game**, then the question, then **Delete** and
  **Cancel**, a centered list, the cursor starting on Cancel; B and MENU are
  Cancel, POWER turns the handheld off with nothing deleted. What is new is
  that the question is a paragraph: it names the game, says what goes with
  it and that it cannot be undone, "Delete Metal Gear Solid? Its saves and
  states, cover and video go too. This cannot be undone." Its lines are
  wrapped to the panel and centered, as the one-line question of the other
  confirms is, in **text-dim** as a question row is, a **menu** note line
  each; a rule (the heading's) divides it from the answers when there is
  room. A question too long for the lines over its answers would leave its
  start where no cursor goes, so a game whose name does not fit is named in
  the heading instead, as on its details page, and the question says "this
  game".
- **The yes** draws one frame, "Deleting..." (11.8), deletes, closes the
  details page onto the shelf as it is now, and says what came of it in a
  one-line message: "Deleted. 1.2 GB given back.", or that some of its
  files would not go, or that nothing was deleted and why.
- **The shelf** changes in place, with no read of the card: the game is off
  its own shelf and Favorites, the cursor on the next game or the one
  before at the end. A shelf left empty goes from the home, as an empty
  system always has, and the home stands on the system in its place; with
  no games left, the empty home (11.19). Favorites keeps the game its
  cursor was on.

**What goes**, every path built from the game's key - its system's folder
and tag, its launch path, its name - and none found by a pattern or a
listing (src/gamedel.h): the game's file, or its folder for a game in one
(a multi-disc set, a ScummVM game); the tracks a `.cue`, `.gdi` or `.toc` at
the top of the folder names, a `.cue`'s `.sbi`, a `.ccd`'s `.img` and
`.sub`; an arcade set's `.zip`, and on NAOMI and Atomiswave the folder of its
name holding its CHD; its saves (`Saves/<stem>.srm`, `.sav`, `.dsv`, and the
arcade cores' own folders); the six state slots and Auto with their
pictures; its cover and video snap, the cover Cover and Video put aside (11.34) and
what a fetch stopped halfway left;
its cached achievement set; and its rows in the library: details and
title, favorite, play sessions. A file another game on the card builds too
stays. A path that would leave `Roms/<console>/`, `Saves/` or the states
folder refuses the whole delete, with nothing touched. Earned achievements
stay: they are the player's.

Host shots: `--info-delete` (the details, the cursor on Delete Game),
`--delete-ask` (the question), `--delete-now` (the shelf after the yes).

### 11.35 File Transfer's heading and steps
The transfer panel (11.3) of USB and Wi-Fi File Transfer, 2026-11-10. Its
heading, in **label** **text-soft**, has the handheld's name on the network
beside its address and the PIN. Over the cable the name comes first:
"http://zyperos.local" on the first line, "10.42.0.1 · PIN 4071" on the
second, since only the computer is on that link. Over Wi-Fi the address
comes first, as it was, and the name beside the PIN: "192.168.1.42" over
"PIN 4071 · zyperos.local", since anyone on the network could answer for a
name, and what a person types should come from the handheld's own screen.
The name over the cable is written with http:// so a browser opens it
rather than searching for it; with a port other than 80 both carry it. With
no name (multicast DNS did not start, or gave the name up after three taken
in a session) or no link (the heading then says "Cable unplugged" or "Wi-Fi
went away"), the heading is the address over the PIN.

Until a browser opens the page, the rows under the rule are the steps of the
connection as the handheld sees it (src/linksteps.h): over the cable
**Cable**, **Address** and **Browser**; over Wi-Fi **Wi-Fi** and **Browser**;
none on the hotspot screen (11.16), which says what it says. A step that is
done has MENU_TICK as its value; the step waited on is a live row, its value
"waiting" (or "unplugged" for a cable with no computer, "not connected" for
no Wi-Fi); the steps after it are dead rows with no value. The rule and the
key legend (11.4) follow, "B: close".

A step that waits too long (the cable 5 seconds with no computer and 10 with
one, the address 10, the browser 30) gets its hint: one sentence saying what
to do about that step, in **menu** **text-dim**, centered and balanced onto
at most three lines, in place of the other steps, under the stalled step's
row and over the rule. Three steps and a three-line hint do not fit under a
two-line heading, and a hint is about one step. The hints name the things a
person can act on: another cable or port, Windows' Device Manager and its
Network adapters, the name and the address to type, a VPN or proxy.

Once a browser has asked, the rows are the usual ones (Browsers, SSH, In /
out, Now); the steps come back if the cable or the Wi-Fi goes. Host shots:
`--hare-steps usb|wifi STATE [noname]`, STATE one of unplugged, nocomputer,
noanswer, nodriver, address, noaddress, browser, nobrowser, nonetwork.

## 12. Copy

- Headings and rows in Title Case; status values lowercase; lines and
  messages in sentence case, ending with a full stop.
- One name for one thing: "needs Wi-Fi" in a value column, "Connect to Wi-Fi
  first." as a message. *(The audit found three names for no network.)*
- Pairs of buttons with a slash and no spaces ("L1/R1", "Left/Right"), chords
  with a plus ("MENU+VOL").
- Counts as "3 / 12"; lists of facts separated by " · ".
- No exclamation marks, except in Zyper's bubble.
- Errors say what happened and what to do, without apology.
- In every language (11.30, `res/strings/GLOSSARY.md`): the same term for
  the same thing; button names as printed (A, START, MENU), the d-pad's
  directions translated; product names never. Title Case is English's:
  Spanish, Portuguese and French write headings and rows in sentence case,
  and status values lowercase as English does; Chinese uses full-width
  punctuation with a space between Chinese and Latin letters or digits;
  French a no-break space before `! ? : ;`. Spanish says *tú*, Portuguese
  *você*, French *vous*.
- No text is cut to fit. A cut word loses its meaning *(
  2026-11-07)*: a narrow place gets a short form of its own that still says
  the thing, in every language (*Transferir USB* on the empty home's card
  and in MENU). A UI string that slides in a marquee is cut too, only
  slowly: a marquee is for names, which are data.

### 11.23 File Transfer while files move
File Transfer is a session of its own, and its screen (the transfer panel,
11.3, or the hotspot screen, 11.16) is a view of it *(2026-11-06:
"maybe instead of closing the channel it should minimize?"; his picks in the
File Transfer 2 lab, `docs/design/mock/transfer2-lab.html`)*:

- **B with nothing moving** closes it, as before. The panel's legend says
  "B: close".
- **B while files move** asks, in three tiles as Computer Connected (11.18),
  heading **Files are still moving**, "Receiving Tobu Tobu Girl.gb" under the
  rule: **Keep going** (two arrows: the screen goes and the files keep moving
  behind the shelf), **Stop after this file** (a page with a tick: nothing
  new starts, the browser is told, and File Transfer closes once the file on
  its way is in) and **Cancel now** (a ring crossed: closed at once; the file
  that was half written is a `.part` file, deleted with its connection, and
  never shows on the shelf as a broken game). Under the tiles, what the
  focused one does; legend "Left/Right: move   A: choose   B: keep going".
  The legend over the panel says "B: keep going or stop", and "B: hide" while
  it closes after the file.
- **MENU** while files move keeps them going and goes to the shelf, as MENU
  goes there from anything.
- **Behind the shelf** it runs from every screen's loop, its mark is in the
  status corner (11.12), and MENU's row under its own name says "on",
  "receiving", "sending" or "closing"; A on it opens the screen again with the
  same PIN. The shelf reads the card again once arrived files have been still
  3 seconds. It closes by itself once the file the question let finish is in,
  when the hotspot stops or the cable has been out 10 seconds, and after half
  an hour with no browser asking anything.
- **A game** while files move asks first, in the same tiles: **Not now**
  (back to the shelf), **Play after this file** (a wait panel, "Finishing
  Tobu Tobu Girl.gb...", then the game) or **Cancel and play**. With nothing
  moving, File Transfer closes for the game and a one-line message says so
  (11.8): "File Transfer closed for the game." A game takes the loop that
  moves the files, and a port restarts the launcher, so the two never run
  together.

Host shots: `--xfer-ask [N] [game]`, tile N focused, and `--status '...
xfer=on'` or `xfer=62` for the corner's mark.
